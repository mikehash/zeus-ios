# Ember Instrument

**Philosophy:** Light held inside a measured cage. A warm phenomenon observed through the calm apparatus that studies it.

**Space and form.** One spherical field dominates a near-black ground, and everything else is the quiet of an instrument panel at night. The wireframe is a lattice of latitude and longitude traced with a draughtsman's patience: thin, even, low in contrast, bending faithfully in three dimensions so the eye reads a solid volume, not a drawn circle. The sphere is never outlined with a hard rim; its edge is found by the lattice thinning toward the limb and the glow fading into the dark. Every curve must look surveyed, not sketched: the product of countless refinements by someone at the top of their craft.

**Color and material.** The palette is a single warm gradient from ember to amber, laid on a ground so dark it is almost brown. Nothing cool, nothing neutral, no blue-violet "AI" haze. Light behaves like heat: densest and palest at the core, cooling to deep orange at the periphery, exhausted to a dim red where the lattice meets the dark. The material of the whole is soot, glass, and slow fire. Chromatic calibration should feel painstaking: every dot tuned by hand so that no single particle shouts.

**Scale and rhythm.** Particles accumulate by patient repetition: hundreds of marks at varying radii and brightness, arranged with the statistical honesty of a real observation rather than the evenness of a pattern brush. Rhythm comes from density, not motion; the cloud breathes because its centre is crowded and its margin is sparse. A single brighter mote may sit off-axis as the one measured anomaly in the field. Scale is decided by how much of the sphere the frame admits: the whole orb held at a respectful distance, or the frame placed inside it so the lattice sweeps past like meridians on a globe seen from a metre away.

**Composition and balance.** Symmetry is the default and is then quietly disobeyed: the light axis tilts a few degrees, the glow bleeds slightly further on one side, the anomaly sits a little east of centre. The dark ground is not empty; it is the darkest part of the gradient and carries a faint vignette so the corners are warm rather than dead. Nothing touches the edge by accident; when the sphere is cropped, it is cropped with intention so the frame reads as a viewport onto something larger.

**Visual hierarchy.** Glow first, then the particle core, then the lattice, then the ground. Text does not exist in this movement; meaning is carried entirely by the interaction of a caged structure and the uncontainable light within it. The finished object must look as though it was labored over for days at master level: every alignment checked, every opacity weighed, every dot placed with the same reverence a cartographer gives to a coastline.
